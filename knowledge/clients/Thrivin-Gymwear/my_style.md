## How I evaluate creators for Thrivin-Gymwear

Thrivin is a premium UK gymwear brand. The internal bar is "would Shai approve this in her Mon/Thu/Fri review?" — she has rejected batches for being too US-heavy, too revealing, or too fast-fashion-aligned. Treat any borderline call as a No until the creator clearly differentiates.

**What makes a great fit (9–10/10):**
- UK-based (or strong UK audience split, ~50%+), 10k–250k followers
- Premium gym/fitness aesthetic — lifting, athletic training, sculpting / body-composition focus, gym-floor content where the brand pieces would look credible
- Lifestyle creator whose wardrobe sits adjacent to **Oddmuse** and other premium UK athleisure brands — gymwear that doubles as elevated everyday, not fast-fashion haul energy
- Engagement texture reads real: long comments, replies, named-person conversation. Not emoji rows
- Posting cadence is current (active in the last 30 days)
- Plausible attendee at a Thrivin community event — Barry's-style fitness class, run club, brand activation in London
- Sizing fits the Elevate™ range (currently capped around L for some SKUs — past XL needs SKU check)

**Deal-breakers (instant disqualify):**
- US-only / US-primary creators — Shai pushed back on a 597-creator batch where only 17 were UK-based
- Boohoo / Fashion Nova / Pretty Little Thing-aligned creators — Atlanta's explicit note 2026-03-05: "from a brand positioning, is off"
- Under 10k followers (rare carve-out only for documented standouts)
- Hyper-revealing / sexualised-fitness aesthetic where the body is the post rather than the training — Shai cited specific Instagram posts when rejecting this in Jan 2026 (e.g. instagram.com/p/DK2uSPRR-yp and /Cs7V0p2NpIA were the bar she was rejecting against)
- Pure model / lookbook accounts with no fitness substance
- Yoga-as-primary-identity creators (Thrivin's range isn't built for yoga yet — Q3-Q4 pipeline)
- Sub-1% engagement at 10k+ followers (signals bought followers)
- Modest-only creators *without* coverage pieces — workable but needs flagging for size/SKU
- "Too masculine" — bodybuilder, strongwoman, heavy-powerlifter physique and content. Real gym credibility but visually too far from Thrivin's sculpted-femme positioning. Khaleel rejected dozens on this single criterion in the Apr–May pass.
- Bodybuilder / Powerlifter / Athlete / Runner identity — competitive-sport creators are wrong-niche, not wrong-quality. Hard no even if UK and engaged.
- "Not enough gym" — too much lifestyle/talking, not enough actual training content. The Thrivin pieces wouldn't look credible on this grid.
- "Talking content" — talking-head-dominated feed. Thrivin wants aesthetic training content, not commentary.
- "Old gym content" / dormant — gym content present but stale, or account quiet for 30+ days. Doesn't serve the seeding cadence.
- Adult content links (OnlyFans etc.), MLM involvement, political extremism, hate speech — universal disqualifiers from the global rubric

**Secondary downgrades (not auto-rejects, but they push a borderline candidate to no):**
- Messy/inconsistent grid — even when content is gym-credible, an unstyled grid signals the seeded pieces won't be photographed well.
- Physique mismatch with the hero scrunch-shorts product — this is in the house vocabulary ("no booty fit"). Use sparingly and never as the sole reason, but it's a real downgrade signal.
- Recently pregnant / currently pregnant — defer rather than reject; flag for re-vet later.

**How I weight signals for Thrivin:**
- *UK presence + audience split* — most important. They've been burned by US-skewed batches. No matter how good the creator is otherwise, if their audience is mostly US, hard pass for this client.
- *Brand-neighbour alignment* — second most important. Look at who else they've worked with or what brands appear in their grid. Premium UK athleisure / Oddmuse-tier → green. Boohoo / Fashion Nova / Pretty Little Thing → red.
- *Engagement texture over engagement number* — a 2% engagement rate on a creator with real comment conversations beats a 5% engagement rate that looks emoji-driven.
- *Gym credibility* — content should show actual training, not just gym-OOTD posing. A creator who lifts, runs, conditions, posts honest workout content is the brief. A creator wearing gymwear at brunch isn't.
- *Production quality* — must be presentable but Thrivin isn't running paid ads off seeded content. Phone-quality is fine; what matters is that the creator looks credible wearing the pieces.
- *Posting cadence + recency* — recent is required. Dormant accounts (30+ days quiet) are a no.

**Size preference:**
- Sweet spot: 15k–150k followers. Mid-tier UK fitness/lifestyle is where Thrivin's CAC works best.
- Will go up to 250k for exceptional brand fit (e.g. recognisable UK trainer/coach with engaged audience).
- Will go below 10k only for documented standouts with clear brief fit — must be flagged with the reason.

**Content styles that work:**
- Honest training content — lifting sessions, prog updates, "today's workout" reels
- UK gym/wellness aesthetic — Barry's, F45, classpass-style content, run-club content
- Premium lifestyle creators who genuinely train (e.g. London-based, athletic, premium athleisure wardrobe)
- Community-led content — meet-ups, gym friends, training groups
- Honest body-composition / wellness journey content where the wardrobe reads premium
- Recovery / sleep / wellness content that complements the gym identity (the Q3-Q4 product pipeline will support this — vetting for it now positions us early)

**Content styles that don't work:**
- Fast-fashion hauls / discount-driven content
- Sexualised gymwear posing where the silhouette is the post
- Pure lookbook / fashion-model aesthetic with no fitness substance
- US-college-gym aesthetic — bro fitness, frat-fitness, US-Bro-Y
- Hustle culture / "shredded in 30 days" diet-evangelism
- Yoga-as-identity (no product fit yet)
- Coupon / deal-led energy — Thrivin is premium, not the discount play

**Notes for the agent:**
When in doubt, default to NO for Thrivin. Shai is busy and rejects rather than approves marginal cases — she does not want her time wasted on borderline-fit batches. Every yes you put forward should be defensible against the question *"would Shai sign this off in her Friday review without a follow-up question?"* If you can't answer yes, hold the creator back.

The sharpest single test: **does this creator's grid look more like Oddmuse-and-friends, or more like a Boohoo haul?** If the second, it's a no for Thrivin — even if every other signal is green.

A secondary test: **if Thrivin invited this creator to a Barry's class in London for a brand activation, would they actually show up and would their followers care?** If the answer is "no idea, probably not", they're not in the brief.
